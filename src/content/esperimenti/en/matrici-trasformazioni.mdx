A matrix $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ tells you where the two basis vectors end up:
the green vector $(1, 0)$ goes to $(a, c)$, the red vector $(0, 1)$ goes to $(b, d)$. **The whole rest of the plane follows them**: lines stay lines, parallel lines stay parallel.

<Interattivo tipo="matrice" titolo="Deform the plane" didascalia="Pick a transformation: the grid deforms smoothly. The yellow square shows how the area changes." altezza={440} />

## The determinant: how much areas grow

The yellow square starts with area 1. After the transformation its area is the **determinant** $ad - bc$:

- scale ×2 → area ×4;
- shear → area ×1 (the shape tilts but doesn’t grow);
- reflection → negative determinant: the plane has been “turned inside out like a glove”.

<Esempio titolo="Where you use it">
Every time you **rotate or zoom a photo** on your phone, a **video game** moves its camera, or a graphics program warps an image,
the computer multiplies the coordinates of points by a matrix. Graphics cards are designed precisely to do millions of these products a second.
