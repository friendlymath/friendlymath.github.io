The number $0.\overline{9}$ (zero point nine recurring, with infinitely many 9s) **is exactly 1**. Not a tiny bit less: precisely 1.
It looks like a trick, but it is one of the most solid equalities in mathematics.

## Proof with fractions

We know that $\tfrac{1}{3} = 0.333\ldots$. Multiply by 3:

$$
3 \times \frac{1}{3} = 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 3 \times 0.333\ldots = 0.999\ldots
$$

## “Algebraic” proof

Let $x = 0.999\ldots$. Then $10x = 9.999\ldots$ Subtracting: $10x - x = 9$, that is $9x = 9$ and $x = 1$.

## The “distance” proof

If two numbers are different, there is at least one other number between them (for example their average). Try to find a number between $0.999\ldots$ and $1$:
there isn’t one. Their distance is smaller than any positive number, so it is **zero**.

<Attenzione>
The idea that there is “a last 9” at infinity is where intuition trips up: the 9s go on for ever, there is no last one. The same goes for $0.24999\ldots = 0.25$.
</Attenzione>
