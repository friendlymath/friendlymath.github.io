In a class of **23** people, what are the chances that at least two share a birthday? Instinct says “tiny”.
Maths says: **about 51%**. With 30 people it goes above 70%, with 50 it gets to around 97%.

## Why intuition gets it wrong

Think about **pairs**, not people. With 23 people there are $\tfrac{23 \times 22}{2} = 253$ possible pairs: plenty of chances for a coincidence.

The clever calculation is the **opposite** one: the probability that all the birthdays are different. The second person must avoid 1 day, the third 2, and so on:

$$
P(\text{all different}) = \frac{365}{365} \cdot \frac{364}{365} \cdot \frac{363}{365} \cdots \frac{343}{365} \approx 0.49
$$

So $P(\text{at least one pair}) \approx 1 - 0.49 = 0.51$.

<Esempio titolo="Try it yourself">
At the next party with more than 23 people, go round asking for birthdays. It’s not guaranteed, but betting on a coincidence is (slightly) favourable.
</Esempio>

The calculation assumes 365 equally likely days. In reality births are not spread perfectly evenly, and that makes coincidences a little more likely, not less.
