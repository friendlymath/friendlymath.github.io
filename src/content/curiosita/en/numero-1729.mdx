The English mathematician **G. H. Hardy** went to visit his Indian colleague **Srinivasa Ramanujan**, who was ill, in a taxi numbered **1729**.
He remarked that it seemed a rather dull number. Ramanujan answered at once that no, it was very interesting:
it is the **smallest number that can be written as the sum of two cubes in two different ways**.

$$
1729 = 1^3 + 12^3 = 9^3 + 10^3
$$

Check: $1 + 1728 = 1729$ and $729 + 1000 = 1729$. ✔️

That’s why 1729 is called the **Hardy–Ramanujan number**, or the “taxicab number”. Hardy himself told the story.

<Curiosita titolo="And the next one?">
The smallest number that is the sum of two positive cubes in **three** different ways is much bigger: 87,539,319. The search for these
“taxicab numbers” has continued with the help of computers.
</Curiosita>
