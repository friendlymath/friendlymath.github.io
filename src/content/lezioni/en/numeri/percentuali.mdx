## Per cent means out of a hundred

The symbol **%** is read “per cent”: $25\% = \tfrac{25}{100} = 0.25$. 100% is the whole, 50% is half, 10% is a tenth.

## Working out a percentage

$p\%$ of a number $N$ is:

$$
\frac{p}{100} \cdot N
$$

20% of 80: $\tfrac{20}{100} \cdot 80 = 16$. Mental trick: you get 10% by moving the decimal point one place ($80 \to 8$); 20% is double that: 16.

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: the test">
You answered **18** questions out of **24** correctly. What percentage is that? $\tfrac{18}{24} = 0.75 = 75\%$.
</Esempio>

## Discounts and increases in one go

- A **20% discount** leaves 80% of the price: multiply by $0.8$. A pair of €60 shoes costs $60 \cdot 0.8 = 48$ €.
- A **20% increase** takes you to 120%: multiply by $1.2$.

<Attenzione distrattore="mercante">
“50% off and then another 50% off: it’s free!” Wrong: the second discount applies to the **already discounted** price.
$100 \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5 = 25$: you still pay €25. The total discount is 75%, not 100%. And a 10% increase followed by a 10% discount does **not** bring you back to the starting price: $100 \cdot 1.1 \cdot 0.9 = 99$.
</Attenzione>
