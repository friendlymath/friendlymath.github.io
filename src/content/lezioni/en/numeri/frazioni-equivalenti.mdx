the third **4 out of 8**. “They ate the same!” I say. Then I correct myself. Then I correct my correction, and I was right the first time. Come on, let’s check together.

## What is a fraction

A fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ says: **split the whole into $b$ equal parts and take $a$ of them**.
The number underneath is the **denominator** (how many parts you cut), the one on top is the **numerator** (how many parts you take).

## Equivalent fractions

Two fractions are **equivalent** when they stand for **the same amount**, even if they are written differently.
If you multiply (or divide) the numerator and the denominator **by the same number**, you get an equivalent fraction:

$$
\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 2}{2 \times 2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{4}{8}
$$

It’s like cutting every slice in half: there are twice as many slices, but the pizza eaten is the same.

## The cross-multiplication trick

To check whether $\dfrac{a}{b}$ and $\dfrac{c}{d}$ are equivalent, just check that $a \times d = b \times c$.
Example: $\dfrac{3}{4}$ and $\dfrac{9}{12}$ → $3 \times 12 = 36$ and $4 \times 9 = 36$. Equal: equivalent!

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: halving a recipe">
A recipe asks for **3/4** of a litre of milk, but your measuring jug only has **1/8** marks.
How many eighths do you need? $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 2} = \dfrac{6}{8}$: fill the jug up to the **sixth mark**.
</Esempio>

<Attenzione distrattore="mercante">
The Merchant offers his favourite rule: “To get an equivalent fraction, **add** the same number on top and bottom!”
Try it: $\dfrac{1}{2} \to \dfrac{1+1}{2+1} = \dfrac{2}{3}$. But 2/3 is more than half a pizza! Adding changes the amount.
Only **multiplying or dividing** by the same (non-zero) number works.
</Attenzione>

<Curiosita>
Simplifying a fraction means finding its “shortest version”: you divide top and bottom by their **greatest common divisor** (GCD, also called the highest common factor).
For example $\dfrac{18}{24}$: the GCD of 18 and 24 is 6, so $\dfrac{18}{24} = \dfrac{3}{4}$.
</Curiosita>
