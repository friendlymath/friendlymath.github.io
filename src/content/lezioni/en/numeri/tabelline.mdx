## Multiplying means adding equal groups

If you have **4 bags** with **3 sweets** each, how many sweets do you have? You can add: $3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12$.
Or, much faster, **multiply**: $4 \times 3 = 12$. The first number says *how many groups*, the second *how many in each group*.

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: eggs">
A box holds **6** eggs. You buy **5** boxes: $5 \times 6 = 30$ eggs. Nobody wants to count them one by one!
</Esempio>

## The times table grid

The table of all the times tables is called the **multiplication grid** (in Italy, the “Pythagorean table”): where the row of 3 meets the column of 4 you find $12$.
Try exploring it: the numbers along the diagonal ($1, 4, 9, 16, 25, \dots$) are the **square numbers**.

## Tricks to remember them

- **Order doesn’t matter**: $3 \times 4 = 4 \times 3$ (commutative property). So there are half as many tables to learn!
- **×10**: add a zero. $7 \times 10 = 70$.
- **×5**: it’s half of ×10. $8 \times 5 = 80 \div 2 = 40$.
- **×9**: $9 \times 7 = 70 - 7 = 63$. And the digits of the answer always add up to 9 ($6 + 3 = 9$).
- **×2**: it’s double. **×4**: double the double.
