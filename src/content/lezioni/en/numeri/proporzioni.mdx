## What is a ratio

The **ratio** of two numbers is their quotient: the ratio of 6 to 3 is $6 : 3 = 2$. It can also be written as a fraction, $\tfrac{6}{3}$.
A **scale** is a ratio: “1 : 25,000” means that 1 cm on the map is 25,000 cm (that is, 250 m) in reality.

## The proportion

A **proportion** is an equality between two ratios:

$$
a : b = c : d \qquad\text{read “a is to b as c is to d”}
$$

$a$ and $d$ are called the **extremes**, $b$ and $c$ the **means**.

## The fundamental property

In every proportion **the product of the means equals the product of the extremes**: $b \cdot c = a \cdot d$.
That’s how you find the missing term: in $3 : 4 = x : 20$ you get $4 \cdot x = 3 \cdot 20$, so $x = 60 \div 4 = 15$.

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: a recipe for more people">
For **4** people you need **300 g** of flour. For **10** people? $4 : 300 = 10 : x \Rightarrow 4x = 3000 \Rightarrow x = 750$ g.
</Esempio>

## Direct and inverse proportion

- **Direct**: if one quantity doubles, the other doubles too (biscuits and flour). The **ratio** stays constant.
- **Inverse**: if one doubles, the other halves (workers and days of work). The **product** stays constant.

<Interattivo tipo="proporzionalita" titolo="Direct or inverse?" didascalia="In direct proportion the ratio stays constant (a straight line); in inverse proportion the product stays constant (the area of the orange rectangle)." altezza={340} />

<Attenzione distrattore="mercante">
“If 3 workers build a wall in 6 days, 6 workers take 12 days: it’s a proportion!” No: more workers, **fewer** days.
It’s **inverse** proportion: $3 \cdot 6 = 6 \cdot x \Rightarrow x = 3$ days. Before setting up a proportion, always ask yourself: “if one goes up, does the other go up or down?”
</Attenzione>
