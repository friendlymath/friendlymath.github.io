## The classical definition

If all outcomes are **equally likely**, the probability of an event is:

$$
P = \frac{\text{favourable outcomes}}{\text{possible outcomes}}
$$

Rolling a die, the probability of a 6 is $\tfrac{1}{6}$. Of an even number ($2, 4, 6$) it is $\tfrac{3}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

## From 0 to 1

- $P = 0$: an **impossible** event (rolling a 7 with one die).
- $P = 1$: a **certain** event (rolling a number between 1 and 6).
- Everything else lies in between. It is often written as a percentage: $\tfrac{1}{2} = 50\%$.

## The complementary event

The probability that an event does **not** happen is $1 - P$. Probability of **not** rolling a 6: $1 - \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{5}{6}$.
It’s a very powerful trick: sometimes it’s easier to work out the opposite, as in the <a href="/en/curiosita/paradosso-del-compleanno/">birthday paradox</a>.

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: two dice">
With two dice there are $6 \times 6 = 36$ possible outcomes. A total of 7 comes up in 6 ways (1+6, 2+5, 3+4, 4+3, 5+2, 6+1): $P = \tfrac{6}{36} = \tfrac{1}{6}$.
A total of 12 in just one way (6+6): $P = \tfrac{1}{36}$. That’s why 7 turns up so often in two-dice games.
</Esempio>

<Attenzione>
Common mistake: “Number 47 hasn’t come up in the lottery for 80 draws, so now it has to!” The draws are **independent**: chance has no memory.
The probability in the next draw is the same as always.
</Attenzione>
