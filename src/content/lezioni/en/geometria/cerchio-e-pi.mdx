## The number π

Take any wheel, measure the distance round it and divide by the diameter: you always get the same number, **π** (pi).

$$
\pi = 3.14159\,26535\ldots
$$

π is **irrational**: its decimal digits never end and never repeat. In calculations we often use $3.14$.

## The circumference

The **circumference** is the “edge” of the circle. Its length is:

$$
C = 2\pi r = \pi d
$$

where $r$ is the radius and $d = 2r$ the diameter.

## The area of a circle

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

One way to see it: cut the circle into lots of thin slices and fit them together, one pointing up and one pointing down. You get almost a rectangle of height $r$ and width half the circumference, $\pi r$. Area: $\pi r \cdot r = \pi r^2$.

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: which pizza is the better deal?">
One **30 cm** pizza or two **20 cm** pizzas? Areas: $\pi \cdot 15^2 \approx 707$ cm² against $2 \cdot \pi \cdot 10^2 \approx 628$ cm².
The big pizza wins: doubling the diameter **quadruples** the area.
</Esempio>

<Attenzione>
Common mistake: using the **diameter** instead of the **radius** in the area formula. With $d = 10$ cm the radius is 5 cm: $A = \pi \cdot 25 \approx 78.5$ cm², not $\pi \cdot 100$.
</Attenzione>
