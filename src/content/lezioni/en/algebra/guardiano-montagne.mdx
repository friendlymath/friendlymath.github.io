## When x is on both sides

Same rule as always: the scales. First **gather the x’s** on one side, then the numbers on the other:

$$
5x + 1 = 2x + 13 \;\Rightarrow\; 5x - 2x = 13 - 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 3x = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; x = 4
$$

With **brackets**, first expand them with the distributive property: $2(x + 3) = 2x + 6$.

<Attenzione distrattore="sirena">
The Siren of Later sings sweetly: “Checking? Oh, you can do it tomorrow…” Checking takes ten seconds and saves you from almost every mistake:
put the value you found back in place of $x$ and check that both sides are equal. $5\cdot4+1 = 21$ and $2\cdot4+13 = 21$. ✔️
</Attenzione>

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: two memberships">
Gym A: **€20** joining fee + **€5** per visit. Gym B: no joining fee, **€9** per visit.
After how many visits do you spend the same? $20 + 5x = 9x \Rightarrow 20 = 4x \Rightarrow x = 5$. From the sixth visit on, gym A is cheaper.
</Esempio>
