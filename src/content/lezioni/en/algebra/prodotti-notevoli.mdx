Grrr. Every day someone turns up with the wrong hammer: “(a + b) squared is a² + b²!” And every day I show them the mirror.

## The square of a binomial

$$
(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2
$$

It reads: **the square of the first, plus twice the product, plus the square of the second**. With a minus: $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$.

## The proof with shapes

Take a square with side $a + b$. Split it with two cuts: you get an $a \times a$ square, a $b \times b$ square and **two rectangles** $a \times b$.
So the total area is $a^2 + b^2 + 2ab$. The two rectangles are exactly the pieces the Merchant “forgets”.

## Difference of two squares

$$
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

The “mixed” terms $+ab$ and $-ab$ cancel out. Very handy for mental arithmetic.

<Esempio titolo="Example">
$21^2 = (20 + 1)^2 = 400 + 40 + 1 = 441$. And $49 \cdot 51 = (50 - 1)(50 + 1) = 2500 - 1 = 2499$. All in your head!
</Esempio>

<Attenzione distrattore="mercante">
“$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$, it’s shorter!” Try it with real numbers: $(3 + 4)^2 = 49$, while $3^2 + 4^2 = 25$. Exactly $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 24$ is missing.
</Attenzione>
