<Scena personaggio="fabbro">
I use this catapult to throw broken hammers over the walls. The stone rises, slows down, falls: it draws a curve called a **parabola**.
The question is always the same: **where does it hit the ground?** To find out you need a quadratic equation. And a formula I’ll forge for you right now.
</Scena>

## The general form

A quadratic equation is written like this:

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0 \qquad (a \neq 0)
$$

## The discriminant

Before solving, work out the **discriminant**:

$$
\Delta = b^2 - 4ac
$$

- $\Delta > 0$: **two** different solutions.
- $\Delta = 0$: **one** solution (two coinciding solutions).
- $\Delta < 0$: **no** solutions among the real numbers.

## The quadratic formula

$$
x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{\Delta}}{2a}
$$

Example: $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$. Here $a = 1, b = -5, c = 6$, so $\Delta = 25 - 24 = 1$ and $x = \tfrac{5 \pm 1}{2}$: the solutions are $x = 3$ and $x = 2$.
Check: $9 - 15 + 6 = 0$ ✔️ and $4 - 10 + 6 = 0$ ✔️.

## Where they meet

The solutions are the points where the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ **touches the x-axis**: two points, just one (it barely touches it) or none (it passes above or below).

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: the fence">
You have 20 m of fencing for a rectangular pen and you want an area of 24 m². If one side is $x$, the other is $10 - x$: $x(10 - x) = 24 \Rightarrow x^2 - 10x + 24 = 0$.
$\Delta = 100 - 96 = 4$, $x = \tfrac{10 \pm 2}{2}$: the pen is $6 \times 4$ m.
</Esempio>

<Attenzione distrattore="barone">
The Baron of Panic sees the formula and shouts: “Too many letters, it’s impossible!” Calm down: it’s just a three-step recipe. **1)** read $a$, $b$, $c$ with their signs; **2)** work out $\Delta$; **3)** put everything into the formula.
The most common mistake is forgetting the sign of $b$: if $b = -5$, then $-b = +5$ and $b^2 = 25$.
</Attenzione>
