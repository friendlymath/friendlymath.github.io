“Bah! Another one who wants to climb the Algebra Mountains with a short sword,” grumbles the Blacksmith.
“Up there things have no names: they’re all called **x**. I’ll forge you one tool, just one: the **scales**.
One single rule, written in fire: *whatever you do to one pan, you do to the other too*.”
</Scena>

## What is an equation

An equation is an **equality with an unknown number**, which we usually call $x$. For example:

$$
3x + 2 = 11
$$

It reads: “three times a number, plus two, makes eleven”. **Solving it** means finding that number.

## The balance principle

Think of the equals sign as a pair of scales in balance. You can do anything you like, **as long as you do it to both pans**:

1. **Take away** 2 from both sides: $3x + 2 - 2 = 11 - 2$, that is $3x = 9$.
2. **Divide** both sides by 3: $x = 3$.
3. **Check**: $3 \cdot 3 + 2 = 11$. ✔️

<Attenzione distrattore="mercante">
“The 2 moves to the other side and **stays +2**!” shouts the Merchant. No: when you “move” a term to the other side of the equals sign, you are really
**taking it away from both pans**, so it changes sign: $3x + 2 = 11 \Rightarrow 3x = 11 - 2$.
Second rotten shortcut: “The 3 moves across and becomes −3!” The 3 multiplies $x$: to remove it you **divide**, $x = 9 \div 3$.
</Attenzione>

<Esempio titolo="Worked example: the taxi">
A taxi costs **€3** to start plus **€2** for every kilometre. You paid **€19**. How many kilometres did you travel?
$2x + 3 = 19 \Rightarrow 2x = 16 \Rightarrow x = 8$. You travelled **8 km**.
</Esempio>

<Storia titolo="Where the word “algebra” comes from">
In the 9th century, in Baghdad, at the court of the caliph al-Ma’mun, the mathematician **al-Khwarizmi** wrote a treatise on equations whose title contains the word
*al-jabr*, “completion”: the operation that removes negative terms from an equation. That’s where **algebra** comes from.
The other operation, *al-muqabala*, “balancing”, removes equal quantities from both sides: exactly our scales.
His name, Latinised as *Algoritmi*, also gave us the word **algorithm**. Read his <a href="/en/storia/al-khwarizmi/">history page</a>.
</Storia>
